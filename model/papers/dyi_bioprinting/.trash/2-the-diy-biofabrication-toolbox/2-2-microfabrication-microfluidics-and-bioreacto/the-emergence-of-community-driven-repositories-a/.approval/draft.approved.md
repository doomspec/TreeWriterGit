The emergence of community-driven repositories and practical fabrication workflows have majorly progressed the field. The Metafluidics platform provided an early model for open-source microfluidics by hosting device designs, assembly instructions, and supporting software in a shared repository, which supported a microfluidic hardware more reproducible and easier to adapt across laboratories. More recently, low-cost and highly reproducible microfabrication pipelines have shown that laboratories without microfabrication background (such as majority of the molecular biology labs) can generate customized microdevices without conventional cleanroom dependence by using accessible workflows that translate CAD designs into functional chips and patterned substrates. Mask-free laser lithography demonstrated that microfluidic chips could be fabricated rapidly and at very low cost using dry-film resist laminated acrylic and laser cutting, with devices reported at roughly \$1 per chip and suitable for droplet generators and mixers. Similarly, an earlier mask-free microfluidic fabrication method used direct laser cutting of paper and plastic films to produce 2D and 3D channels without photomasks or cleanrooms, demonstrating how simple fabrication workflows can replace resource-intensive soft lithography-based methodologies. More recently, homebrew photolithography has shown that master molds and elastomeric devices can be produced outside a cleanroom using household appliances and improvised equipment, further lowering the barrier to rapid prototyping in microfluidics. These approaches are especially valuable for tissue engineering as they reduce the technical distance between a biological question and the device needed to study it.
