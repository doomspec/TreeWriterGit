As an essential part of tissue engineering toolbox, microfabrication and microfluidics provide precise control over cellular organization, microscale geometry, and biochemical transport in engineered tissues. In contrast to fully closed commercial workflows, recent open-source and low-cost approaches have begun to lower the barrier to entry for labs that lack cleanroom access or dedicated microfabrication infrastructure. As fabrication depends on access to microfabricated tools as much as on access to cells or biomaterials, this democratization effort is particularly important for organ-on-chip platforms, perfusable constructs, and bioreactor-enabled tissue models.
