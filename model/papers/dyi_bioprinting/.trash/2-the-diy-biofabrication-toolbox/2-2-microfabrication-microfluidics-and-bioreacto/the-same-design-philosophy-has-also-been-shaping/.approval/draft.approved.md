The same design philosophy has also been shaping organ-on-chip systems. The recent STARTER platform introduces standardized microfluidic building blocks and a fluidic circuit board to support interoperable, swappable organ-on-chip modules that are compatible with standard lab automation hardware. That modularity turns organ-on-chip from a one-off custom device into a reconfigurable platform that can be adapted to multiple tissues, sensors, and experimental formats. In the context of a democratization perspective, STARTER is an example of how open design principles can reduce the dependence of organ-on-chip research on specialized engineering support and proprietary integration workflows.
