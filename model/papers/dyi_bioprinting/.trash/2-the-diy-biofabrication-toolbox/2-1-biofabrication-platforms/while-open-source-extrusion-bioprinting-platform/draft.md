While open-source extrusion bioprinting platforms have become increasingly mature, similar efforts in droplet-based systems remain limited. This is largely due to the reliance on proprietary actuation technologies such as piezoelectric printheads and stringent fluidic control requirements. When it comes to light-based bioprinting, although many commercial systems remain proprietary, the field is moving toward more open and modular implementations. As an example, the mSLAb platform presents a fully open-source masked stereolithography (mSLA) bioprinter built from a consumer-grade desktop printer with minimal modifications on temperature and humidity control for cell-compatible hydrogels. Similarly, Sanchez-Garcia et al. developed an open-source digital light processing (DLP) bioprinter using off-the-shelf optical components, Arduino-based control, and a custom Python interface.
