Open-source hardware and software ecosystems have been getting broader interest in tissue engineering field. By reducing the cost for both developers and users, DIY toolkits enable tissue engineering work in smaller or less-funded environments which would not be able to afford some of the commercial equipment. Moreover, transparency and reproducibility of the research also enhance as scientists can inspect, modify, and share designs rather than trying to navigate within black-box systems. Broader access to these DIY biofabrication ecosystems also boost faster prototyping, local customization, and parallel development across many groups. Rather than only a few vendors or elite laboratories individually growing, collaborations through DIY ecosystems enable collective problem-solving for the tissue engineering bottlenecks. Democratization also has a training and community effect. Students and early-career researchers can learn biofabrication through buildable, modular systems, which expands the future workforce.
