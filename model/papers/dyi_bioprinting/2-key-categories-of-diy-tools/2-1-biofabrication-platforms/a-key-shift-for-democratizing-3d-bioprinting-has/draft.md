A key shift for democratizing 3D bioprinting has been the emergence of open-source printheads and printer conversions that retrofit widely available FDM printers into 3D bioprinters for extrusion-based bioprinting (EBB). Sanz-Garcia et al shared one of the first open-source printheads, which can be mounted on low-cost desktop 3D printers to create a microextrusion bioprinter with temperature control, compatibility with multiple bioink formulations, and high cell viability. The authors further demonstrated that the same printhead could be transferred across different printer platforms, which prevented the restriction of the designed printhead into one 3D printer type. While lots of studies demonstrated the conversion of plastic printers into 3D bioprinters, Gomes Gama et al. extended this design approach further by building a significantly low-cost 3D bioprinter from recycled scrap material, exemplifying that functional bioprinting platforms can even be assembled from repurposed components rather than specialized commercial parts.
