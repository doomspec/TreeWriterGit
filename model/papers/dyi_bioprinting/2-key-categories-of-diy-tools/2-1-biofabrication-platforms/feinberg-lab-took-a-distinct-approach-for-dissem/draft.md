Feinberg Lab took a distinct approach for disseminating their high-performance open-source 3D bioprinter conversion. Rather than relying solely on conventional publication strategies, they have organized annual, internationally attended workshops in which participants constructed their own bioprinters, received hands-on training in Freeform Reversible Embedding of Suspended Hydrogels (FRESH) technique, and then took the bioprinters to their respective institutions for continued use in research. This iterative, user-centered approach has enabled the lab to continuously refine their bioprinter conversion process, ensuring that both the hardware designs and accompanying protocols are accessible and practical for users with diverse technical backgrounds and levels of expertise. Engberg et al. further advanced this direction by introducing an open-source extrusion bioprinter built on the E3D motion system and tool changer architecture, explicitly enabling FRESH and multimaterial bioprinting in a modular and reproducible format.
