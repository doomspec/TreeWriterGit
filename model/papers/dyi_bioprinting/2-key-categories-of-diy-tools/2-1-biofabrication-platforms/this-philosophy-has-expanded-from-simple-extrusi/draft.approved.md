This philosophy has expanded from simple extrusion heads to integrated, function-oriented platforms. The MOS3S platform combines a low-cost desktop printer with open-source syringe pumps and microfluidic printheads to support coaxial deposition, in situ crosslinking, and hierarchical tissue fabrication at a reported hardware cost of under €700. The open-source multihead bioprinter from Chimene et al. similarly shows how cost-effective commercial printers can be converted into customizable multi-extrusion systems for broader rheological and architectural control. Krige et al. added another important design strategy by modifying a low-cost bio-printer to switch between plastic and gel extrusion, showing how a single platform can be adapted for both structural thermoplastic fabrication and hydrogel deposition without requiring a fully proprietary system.
