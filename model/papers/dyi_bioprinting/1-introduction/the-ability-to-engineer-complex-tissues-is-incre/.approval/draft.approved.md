Tissue engineering has become a central goal of modern biomedical research. Human-relevant tissue models have a demand across regenerative medicine, disease modelling, and drug discovery [@hofer_engineering_2021; @ingber_human_2022; @low_organs-on-chips_2021]. Recent advances in biofabrication have introduced powerful innovative capabilities for constructing architecturally complex, multi-material, and functional living tissues[@daly_bioprinting_2021; @levato_volumetric_2025; @cooke_biofabrication_2025; @huang_organoid_2025; @editorial_pushing_2025]. However, engineering tissues that approach the complexity, organization, and function of native organs remains difficult [@he_high-resolution_2024; @editorial_pushing_2025], and requires a combination of engineering control and intrinsic self-assembly [@zhao_integrating_2024; @dettinger_open-source_2022]. Meeting these engineering demands increasingly depends on access to sophisticated biofabrication technologies and the ability to integrate them into reproducible experimental workflows.
