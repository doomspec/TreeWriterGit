The ability to engineer complex tissues is increasingly shaped not only by biological insight, but also by access to specialized fabrication and automation technologies. Recent advances in biofabrication have introduced powerful innovative capabilities for constructing architecturally complex, multi-material, and functional living tissues. In order to achieve the holy grail of fully functional and vascularized tissue replacements, a hybrid approaches bringing together different biofabrication techniques, microfluidic systems and automated liquid handling platforms are needed. While biofabrication techniques such as 3D bioprinting would provide the attainment of architectural intricacy of native tissues, microfabrication and microfluidic techniques would enable integration of sensors and maturation of tissues within bioreactors. In addition to them, automated liquid handling would alleviate the labour-intensive, difficult to scale steps of cell culture and tissue handling. However, many of these enabling technologies remain restricted to well-funded laboratories due to high material costs, proprietary hardware/software, and specialized manufacturing requirements with specialized expertise. As a result, access to state-of-the-art tissue engineering capabilities is unevenly distributed, which eventually limits innovation, reproducibility, and global participation in the field.
