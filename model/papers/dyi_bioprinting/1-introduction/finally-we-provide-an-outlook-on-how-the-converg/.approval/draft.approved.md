Looking forward, the convergence of DIY biofabrication with artificial intelligence, self-driving laboratories, modular robotics, and digital twin technologies may fundamentally reshape how engineered tissues are developed and shared. As experimental hardware, software, and biological workflows become increasingly interoperable, researchers will be able to build reusable and continuously improving biofabrication pipelines rather than isolated laboratory solutions. We anticipate that democratizing access to these technologies will accelerate innovation, improve reproducibility, broaden global participation, and ultimately advance the engineering of increasingly complex human tissues.
