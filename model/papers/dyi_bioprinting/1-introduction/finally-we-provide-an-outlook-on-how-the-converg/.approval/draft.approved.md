The convergence of DIY biofabrication with artificial intelligence (AI), modular robotics, digital twins, and self-driving laboratories creates new opportunities for autonomous bioengineering By promoting open hardware, interoperable software, standardized protocols, and shared engineering knowledge, it provides a framework for building reproducible and adaptable biofabrication platforms. As experimental hardware, software, and biological workflows become increasingly interoperable, researchers will be able to build reusable and continuously improving biofabrication pipelines rather than isolated laboratory solutions. Democratizing both technologies and expertise has the potential to accelerate technology translation, improve reproducibility, and enable broader participation in engineering increasingly sophisticated human tissues.
