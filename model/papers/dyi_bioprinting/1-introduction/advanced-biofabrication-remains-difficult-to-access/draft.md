Developing a complete biofabrication workflow requires expertise that spans biology, mechanical engineering, microfabrication, robotics, software development, automation, and data science [@editorial_pushing_2025; @huang_organoid_2025; @lo_review_2024]. Such expertise rarely sits within a single laboratory. Individual technologies contribute different capabilities to tissue engineering, but advanced biofabrication depends on their convergence into coordinated experimental systems rather than on any single instrument or method alone [@editorial_pushing_2025; @huang_organoid_2025]. Biofabrication techniques provide architectural control over cells and biomaterials [@dhand_lithography-based_2025]. Microfabrication and microfluidic techniques enable physiologically relevant culture environments and integration of sensors., whereas automated liquid handling supports reproducible and scalable tissue manufacturing [@huang_organoid_2025; @zhao_integrating_2024; @dettinger_open-source_2022]. Together, these technologies enable increasingly sophisticated tissue models. However, integrating them into a functional biofabrication workflow typically requires extensive customization, multidisciplinary expertise, and a detailed understanding of how independently developed technologies interact [@huang_organoid_2025; @zhao_integrating_2024]. Overcoming these barriers will require more than incremental advances in individual technologies. It will require approaches that democratize both the tools and the knowledge needed to build interoperable biofabrication ecosystems [@maia_chagas_haves_2018].
