In this perspective, we discuss how the growing “do-it-yourself” (DIY) ecosystem is changing the toolkit of tissue engineers by lowering the barriers to entry for advanced experimental workflows. We highlight three key categories of emerging DIY tools, including open-source biofabrication platforms such as 3D bioprinting, accessible microfabrication and microfluidic approaches, and low-cost liquid handling setups within the automation systems. We further examine how these technologies are accelerating democratization by enabling laboratories to build customizable, modular, and scalable experimental infrastructure at a fraction of the cost of commercial systems. Beyond cost reduction, DIY ecosystem also promotes rapid prototyping, hardware transparency, community-driven innovation, and the development of adaptable experimental pipelines tailored to specific biological questions.
