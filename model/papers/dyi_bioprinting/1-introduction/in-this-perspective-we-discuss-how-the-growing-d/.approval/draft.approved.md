In this Perspective, we propose that democratizing biofabrication requires broadening access not only to hardware, but also to software, protocols, engineering expertise, and integrated experimental workflows [@maia_chagas_haves_2018; @wilkinson_fair_2016; @teytelman_protocols.io_2016]. We discuss how the emerging “do-it-yourself” (DIY) ecosystem is expanding the capabilities available to tissue engineers by making advanced technologies easier to build, adapt, and integrate [@pearce_building_2012; @diederich_versatile_2020]. We highlight three key categories of emerging DIY tools, including open-source biofabrication platforms such as 3D bioprinters, accessible microfabrication and microfluidic approaches, and modular laboratory automation, including low-cost liquid handling systems.  Their value extends beyond reducing cost. Open designs, shared protocols, interoperable software, community-developed hardware, and machine-readable workflows make it easier to reproduce, modify, and build upon existing technologies, allowing experimental platforms to evolve through collaborative improvement instead of isolated development [@pearce_building_2012; @wilkinson_fair_2016; @teytelman_protocols.io_2016; @ouyang_open-source_2021]. We argue that this shift from proprietary, laboratory-specific solutions toward open and reusable biofabrication infrastructure will accelerate innovation, improve reproducibility, and broaden participation in advanced tissue engineering.
