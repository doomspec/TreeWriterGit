In this Perspective, we propose that democratizing biofabrication requires broadening access not only to hardware, but also to software, protocols, engineering expertise, and integrated experimental workflows [@maia_chagas_haves_2018; @wilkinson_fair_2016; @teytelman_protocols.io_2016]. We discuss how the emerging do-it-yourself (DIY) biofabrication movement is expanding the capabilities available to tissue engineers by making advanced technologies easier to build, adapt, and integrate [@pearce_building_2012; @diederich_versatile_2020]. The Perspective focuses on three areas in which DIY approaches are having the greatest impact: open-source biofabrication platforms, accessible microfabrication and microfluidic technologies, and modular laboratory automation, including low-cost liquid handling systems. Together, these developments enable laboratories to assemble biofabrication platforms tailored to their scientific questions rather than relying exclusively on commercial solutions. Their value extends beyond reducing cost. Open designs, shared protocols, interoperable software, community-developed hardware, and machine-readable workflows make it easier to reproduce, modify, and build upon existing technologies, allowing experimental platforms to evolve through collaborative improvement instead of isolated development [@pearce_building_2012; @wilkinson_fair_2016; @teytelman_protocols.io_2016; @ouyang_open-source_2021]. We argue that this shift from proprietary, laboratory-specific solutions toward open and reusable biofabrication infrastructure will accelerate innovation, improve reproducibility, and broaden participation in advanced tissue engineering [@editorial_pushing_2025].
