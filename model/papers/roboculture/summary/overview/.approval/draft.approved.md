RoboCulture is a flexible robotic platform for automated biological experimentation that integrates liquid handling, perception, and reactive control to support extended autonomous cell-culture workflows. Built around a general-purpose manipulator, the system combines precise robotic pipetting with a Digital Pipette v2 that enables reliable force-feedback tip pickup and disposal, thereby maintaining small-volume transfer performance while supporting uninterrupted multi-step operation. To close the sensing loop, RoboCulture estimates culture optical density directly from camera images, allowing noncontact growth monitoring and data-driven detection of subculturing time points without reliance on a conventional plate reader. These hardware and perception modules are coordinated through a behavior-tree control architecture that modularizes core actions, conditions, and recovery behaviors, enabling robust experiment execution in the presence of changing system state and long-horizon task dependencies. Together, these contributions address a central challenge in self-driving laboratories by coupling adaptable robotic manipulation with online biological measurement, and we validate the resulting platform in a 15-hour autonomous yeast-culture experiment in which RoboCulture tracked growth and executed subculturing decisions across replicate cultures without operator intervention.
