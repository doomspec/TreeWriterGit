Several limitations of the present system should be acknowledged. First, the robustness of the visual-servoing pipeline depends on accurate perception of wells, tips, and other workspace features; when image-based localization fails or degrades, the resulting control corrections can produce unintended motion and reduce task reliability. Second, although the platform automates downstream culture maintenance, the overall workflow still requires a manual seeding step, so the current implementation does not yet constitute a fully end-to-end autonomous biological experiment. Third, experimental validation in this study was limited to yeast culture, and the extent to which the same hardware and control architecture will generalize to other organisms, media properties, vessel formats, or sterility constraints remains to be established. Finally, while the force-feedback tip exchange mechanism performed reliably under the conditions tested, its behavior in more extreme operating regimes, including increased wear, larger alignment disturbances, or more variable consumables, warrants further characterization. Addressing these limitations will require improved perception robustness, automation of inoculation, broader biological benchmarking, and additional stress testing of the tool-changing subsystem.
