RoboCulture makes three primary contributions to the development of self-driving laboratory infrastructure for biology. First, we introduce a vision-based liquid-handling framework that closes the control loop with real-time visual feedback, enabling the system to detect liquid levels and adapt pipetting actions during execution rather than relying solely on open-loop actuation. Second, we present Digital Pipette v2, an open and extensible successor to the original Digital Pipette platform, which incorporates interchangeable tips to broaden the range of supported transfer tasks while preserving the precision and modularity required for automated experimentation. Third, we demonstrate the integration of these hardware capabilities in a 15-h autonomous yeast-culture experiment orchestrated with a behavior-tree control architecture, showing how composable robotic decision logic can coordinate long-duration biological workflows with minimal human intervention.
