Pipetting performance was evaluated using an ISO 8655-inspired gravimetric protocol analogous to that used for the original Digital Pipette platform @digital-pipette, with emphasis on both delivery precision and the robustness of autonomous consumable handling. Across the tested transfer conditions, the RoboCulture digital pipette consistently achieved a coefficient of variation below 2% for dispensed volume, satisfying the principal performance criterion for reliable small-volume liquid handling and indicating that robotic actuation did not introduce substantial variability beyond that expected for benchtop precision pipetting. Autonomous tip exchange was likewise highly reliable, with successful pickup and disposal maintained throughout repeated transfer cycles, thereby demonstrating that liquid-handling accuracy was not obtained at the expense of operational continuity. When benchmarked against manual pipetting by trained personnel, the robotic system produced comparable volume-delivery performance while offering the additional advantage of repeatable execution without operator fatigue, supporting its suitability as a core liquid-handling module for extended self-driving laboratory workflows.
