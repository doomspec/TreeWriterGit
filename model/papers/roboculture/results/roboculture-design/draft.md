The robotic culture platform was implemented as a Franka-based workstation organized around a central home pose beneath an overhead camera, from which the manipulator could repeatedly localize and access each hardware element in the workspace. Static devices and labware were localized using AprilTags @olson2011tags, which provided a practical and sufficiently accurate reference framework for equipment whose operation did not demand submillimeter placement. When the physical arrangement of the workspace changed, the system was recalibrated by measuring the pixel offsets between each AprilTag and the desired end-effector pose for the corresponding object, thereby restoring accurate motion planning and transfer between hardware items without redesigning the broader workflow. Well plates constituted an important exception to this strategy: because pipetting into individual wells required substantially higher positioning precision, AprilTags were not placed on the plates themselves, and plate handling relied on a more accurate alignment procedure tailored to that requirement. This design was validated progressively, first through optimization of core robotic functions, including pipetting, vision-based control, and optical-density perception, and subsequently through demonstration of the fully integrated platform in a 15-hour autonomous yeast culturing experiment conducted on a 96-well plate.
