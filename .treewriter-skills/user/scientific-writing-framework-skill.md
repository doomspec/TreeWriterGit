# Multi-Level Framework for Scientific Writing

Analyse, write, and edit manuscripts at four hierarchical levels. **Write top-down, edit bottom-up** — fix the story before polishing words.

| Level | Focus | Key question |
|-------|-------|--------------|
| **Manuscript** | Story arc, overall narrative | Why? What? So what? |
| **Section** | Function of each part | Does each section do its job? |
| **Paragraph/Sentence** | Flow, coherence, signposting | Is the logic visible? |
| **Word** | Economy, precision, formality | Is every word earning its place? |

Levels 2–4 tables, checklists, and tense rules: **`scientific-writing-framework-reference.md`**.

## Level 1 — Manuscript: the story

Every paper answers: **Why?** (problem, gap) → **What?** (what you did and found) → **So what?** (implications).

**Four-sentence exercise** (write before drafting):
1. **Background:** why is this work interesting?
2. **Open question** ("However, …"): what is the roadblock?
3. **Contribution** ("Here, …"): what gap does this fill?
4. **Result and significance:** what can we now do that we could not before?

**Hourglass structure:** broad field → your specific contribution → broader implications.

**Abstract:** self-contained miniature (background + gap + contribution + significance). Main findings stated, not hinted.

**Figures carry the story** — overview (system/approach), results (substantiate claims), outlook (implications). Paper message legible from figures alone.

**Reader hierarchy:**

| Reader | Reads | Needs |
|--------|-------|-------|
| Quick | Title, abstract, figures | Complete story, compressed |
| General | + Introduction, conclusions | Context and significance |
| Specialist | Full text | Methods, detail, nuance |

**SI reproducibility test:** could a strong MSc/first-year PhD reproduce the work from SI + repo? Paper-specific knowledge belongs in SI, not the main text.

**Navigability:** descriptive section headings; leading sentences signal each paragraph's point; figure-caption titles summarise findings.
