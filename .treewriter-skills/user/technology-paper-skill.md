# Technology Paper Skill

For papers whose contribution is a **technology** (instrument, algorithm, software, assay, device, pipeline). Make the technology the star, prove its advance against the state of the art, and make it usable and reproducible.

**Core principle:** A technology paper that only describes a method (without comparison, characterization, and reproducibility) reads as an engineering report, not a contribution.

---

## Checklist (Level 1)

Before saving or approving a technology-focused unit:

1. **Star framing** — title, abstract, and Fig 1 name the technology and its key innovation (not just the application).
2. **Conceptual advance** — one quotable sentence states what was not possible before.
3. **Direct comparison** — head-to-head benchmark on shared data vs real alternatives.
4. **Pros and cons** — quantified strengths **and** limits; no pros-only characterization.
5. **Claim ↔ demo** — each headline claim has a demonstration sized to it.
6. **Reproducibility** — code, data, protocols, or availability statement is explicit.
7. **Venue fit** — structure and evidence bar match 3–5 recent papers in the target journal.
8. **No hype** — big-picture framing stays within what the data support.

---

## When to use

- Drafting or revising **Introduction**, **Results**, or **Discussion** units for a methods/tool paper.
- Reviewing whether a section sells the technology or buries it under biology.
- Pair with **scientific-writing-framework-skill** for paragraph-level flow and hourglass structure.

## Completion criterion

Each technology unit you touch should leave at least one **checkable** improvement: explicit conceptual-advance sentence, quantified comparison, stated limit, or claim-to-demo mapping — not vague "improve framing" notes.
